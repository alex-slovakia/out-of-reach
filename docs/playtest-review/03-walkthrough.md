# Page 3 — Final intended game walkthrough
This describes the agreed target, not the current I04 build.

1. **Living opening menu.** The real ship/dock scene sits behind sparse typography, slow camera drift and understated sound. Hover responds clearly. Practice and 60-second loading are understandable without a long instruction wall.
2. **Coastal loading base.** On a compact sea-side loading deck, choose food, water/oxygen reserves, energy equipment, spare parts and science cargo. Physically lift one case with two hands, follow the bay marker and secure it. Heavy cases slow movement and increase exertion. The mass/budget choices have later consequences. White countdown turns red for the last ten seconds; this is a cargo deadline.
3. **Boarding and ascent.** When loading closes, the secured manifest is locked. Show hatch closure, seating/restraint, checklist and ignition through a short cinematic time jump. A smaller fictional crew/cargo vehicle rides a rocket into orbit and docks with AURORA-7, already assembled there. The ocean setting is original fiction, not a claim NASA launches our whole large habitat this way.
4. **Departure and apparent calm.** Inside AURORA-7, look down to see the appropriate body, move naturally, inspect supplies and physical screens. Gentle cabin music and routine communication make the space feel inhabited. A chapter transition establishes the later Mars-bound mission epoch; orbital launch scenery and deep-space ephemeris are not conflated.
5. **The disturbance.** A damaged system produces a warning, disrupted machinery and coherent light/sound escalation. The calm score stops. The protagonist calls for contact; a damaged link leaves silence. Keep movement where safe, then give a clear SURVIVE objective. Deep-space Earth messages obey modeled delay.
6. **Stabilize the ship.** Identify the visible fault, select a repair, physically operate a control/tool and restore power and life support. Use the actual resource/repair ledger. Concise statuses, readable pictograms and breathing carry urgency; not a stack of duplicate orange messages.
7. **Prepare for EVA.** Inspect/don the suit; the visible body changes. Enter the airlock, close the inner hatch, follow suit/pressure checks and depressurize only the chamber. The physical display counts down. Latches, valves and suit sounds communicate the sequence.
8. **Repair the antenna outside.** Attach the locking tether. Traverse smoothly with hand-over-hand contact while free-looking. See the fault, brace with one hand, use the tool with the other and complete a short authoritative repair action. Coherent sunlight and a task lamp reveal the hull, dish and tools. Return and repressurize; no cabin venting or teleport.
9. **Restore contact.** Sit at the radio station and send the distress message. The measured reference range determines signal time; processing is separate. A later incoming message confirms reception. CONTACT RESTORED is a dramatic midpoint, not the win screen.
10. **Keep the ship alive.** Eat and drink actual supplies; manage oxygen, power and needs. Build one useful grow rack from real parts, plant a scenario-provided seedling and tend it through simulated growth days. A physical day display follows mission time. Sleep in a berth advances the same model and can be interrupted by danger or a message. The loading choices now matter.
11. **Information and uncertainty.** Use the physical touchscreen with a mouse-guided finger, tactile touch response and labeled Earth imagery/navigation reference. It shows observation date and assumptions rather than invented live weather/GPS. A fictional nearby rescue plan may suffer a setback, forcing another survival decision; exact dates require an internally consistent scenario.
12. **Success or failure.** Successful management lasts until an actual later rendezvous, rescue and time-separated Mars arrival/departure sequence. Failure follows the real failed survival state with an appropriate restrained sequence. Neither outcome is generated merely by clicking the antenna.
13. **Your mission record.** Over the final scene, an optional skippable/pausable scroll tells what you packed, how choices affected the mission, key events and the NASA sources used. Separate source data, derived physics and fictional assumptions. Detailed numbers remain accessible in Flight log, not forced into a packed modal.
