OUT OF REACH
The mission you design is the mission you must survive.

A first-person space-survival game for Windows, made during the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026
hackathon (14-15 November 2026), challenge "Space Mission Design Game", by team Polish Alex Goats
(Alexander Kozovy, Alex (Apoliak)).


PLAY IT
1. Download the ZIP file from this folder (OUT-OF-REACH ... Windows_x64.zip) and unzip it anywhere.
2. Run the game's .exe file in the unzipped folder. No installation, no account and no network
   connection are needed. If Windows SmartScreen warns about an unsigned app, choose
   "More info", then "Run anyway".
3. Controls are shown in the pause menu (Esc). On foot: WASD to move, Shift to sprint, mouse to look,
   E to interact, Tab for ship systems. In zero gravity your hands and feet push you off surfaces.
4. Choose "Start timed - 60 s" for the real challenge or "Practice - no deadline" to learn the pad.
   A full mission takes about half an hour. An option for reduced camera motion and light effects
   is on the first menu.


THE MISSION
You are Houston, the only crew member of AURORA-7, a small fictional research ship bound for Mars.

1. Design. At launch complex LC-7 you carry the cargo yourself: oxygen, water, food, batteries, solar
   wings, spare parts and a 90 kg science spectrometer. Limits: 600 kg payload, 800 credits, power load
   versus generation, supplies for 30 crew-days, and 60 seconds in the timed mode.
2. Launch to a real NASA countdown on the radio. Later, deep in space, a debris strike: power 20 %,
   life support and antenna down.
3. Repair with the parts and energy you packed. Every prompt shows the exact cost; a refused action
   costs nothing.
4. Spacewalk to the antenna: suit, airlock, tether rope, 3 hours of suit oxygen.
5. Call Earth once. The reply cannot arrive faster than light: 31 min 59 s of mission time on day 1,
   and still over 30 minutes on day 9.
6. Survive 30 days in chapters (days 1, 5, 9, 14, 20, 30): ration pouches, sleep strapped into the
   berth, ordinary days as a short montage, radio traffic on a handheld radio, hard choices, and a
   rescue that is never instant.
7. The outcome: a rescue craft docks before your eyes under a real star field, or the loss names
   its cause.
8. Read the record: why you lived or died, with the science behind each consequence. Then try
   another design.


NASA DATA INSIDE THE GAME
- NASA/JPL Horizons Earth-Mars ephemeris (14 Nov - 15 Dec 2026, packaged offline, SHA-256 checked):
  distance divided by the speed of light, both ways, plus 10 minutes of processing = the earliest
  reply; it also times the day-14 message, and the range and the one-way signal time stay on
  screen during play.
- NASA Life Support Baseline Values (BVAD, NASA/TP-2015-218570/REV2): 0.84 kg oxygen, 1.01 kg CO2,
  2.5 kg water and 1.83 kg food per crew-day; cabin CO2 levels; spacewalk costs.
- NASA EPIC / NOAA DSCOVR: the real Earth image on the cockpit terminal, observed 11 Nov 2026 and
  shown with its age.
- NASA GIBS / ESDIS, MODIS on Terra: the inset on the same terminal, Slovakia on 14 Nov 2026.
- NASA audio, Apollo 11 launch commentary (two excerpts; the mission name is omitted): the radio
  countdown of the fictional launch.
- NASA crew practice (Crew Systems, Medical Operations, Food in Space, Artemis II menu, Veggie):
  berth restraints, 8.5 hours of maximum rest, ready-to-eat, rehydratable and thermostabilized food,
  the optional grow module.
- NASA SVS Deep Star Maps 2020 (Gaia DR2: ESA/Gaia/DPAC): the space sky, shown brighter in the
  rescue finale.
- NASA/JPL-Caltech Viking Mars map: Mars in the recovery cinematic.
- NASA Deep Space Network sites (Goldstone, Madrid, Canberra; coordinates from NASA/JPL Horizons):
  the line that names the complex with Mars in view.
- NASA/JPL Mars Science Laboratory RAD (an average of 1.8 mSv per day measured on the cruise to
  Mars): the radiation estimate shown during play, for information only.

What is fiction: AURORA-7, LC-7, the crew, the rescue, the prices and the time compression (always
shown on screen). The Mars-centre distance is a labelled stand-in for the ship's position, not a
trajectory. This is an educational game, not NASA-certified software. No NASA logos or insignia are
used and no endorsement is implied. We acknowledge the use of imagery provided by services from
NASA's Global Imagery Browse Services (GIBS), part of NASA's Earth Science Data and Information
System (ESDIS).


UNDER THE HOOD
Unity 6000.3.28f1 (URP), C#. One deterministic mission simulation: one clock, one resource ledger in
real units, all-or-nothing spending; the visuals only show committed state. All 544 automated
EditMode tests and 178 PlayMode scene tests pass on the submitted build, plus scripted full-mission
runs and repeated human playtests. Original 3D models and textures are generated by Python scripts
for Blender and NumPy/Pillow.
