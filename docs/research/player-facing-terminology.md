# Player-facing terminology

Decision: Alex, 14 November 2026, approximately 19:00 CET. Explain the mission without assuming spaceflight knowledge. The game is English; detailed technical information remains available voluntarily.

| Term | Meaning and first introduction |
| --- | --- |
| Houston | The fictional playable astronaut's personal name. First speaker label: **HOUSTON · YOU**; subsequent personal lines: **HOUSTON**. This is not a label for ground control. |
| AURORA-7 | The fictional spacecraft you occupy. Introduce as **AURORA-7 · YOUR SHIP**. It is not Earth or the name of an onboard computer. |
| Mission Control | The team on Earth. Speaker label: **MISSION CONTROL · EARTH**. Mark received communications as delayed when relevant. |
| Ship computer | Local onboard status voice. Label: **SHIP COMPUTER**. It may immediately warn about a power fault or trajectory deviation. |
| Spacewalk (EVA) | First use spells out the action. EVA means extravehicular activity. In this mission it means leaving the pressurized ship in a spacesuit. Later objectives can simply say **Go outside** or **Return to the airlock**. |
| Airlock | The two-door pressure chamber between the cabin and space. Teach through the actual close-door, suit-check and pressure sequence. |
| Life support | The ship systems providing breathable conditions. Distinguish stored oxygen from removal of exhaled carbon dioxide when an action requires that distinction. |

## Dialogue direction

Houston's crisis call should identify both parties naturally: “Mission Control, this is Houston aboard Aurora Seven. We're drifting off course. Can you hear me?” Performance instructions belong in production notes, not spoken words or subtitle parentheses. The exact accepted recording and subtitle must match.

Do not reuse the older “Aurora Seven, Houston. Do you copy?” draft as a ground response or as Houston trying to call Earth; it leaves the intended recipient unclear. It is excluded from the active scene binding.

Local speech does not imply an immediate ground reply. The existing communications service remains the authority for outbound travel, ground processing and inbound travel.

## Primary references

- [NASA: What Is a Spacewalk?](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/for-kids-and-students/what-is-a-spacewalk-grades-5-8/) — EVA terminology and the spacesuit/spacewalk context.
- [NASA: Spacewalk Spacesuit Basics](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/johnson/spacewalk-spacesuit-basics/) — suit radio communication with crewmates and mission control.
- [NASA: Spaceflight Q&A](https://robotics.nasa.gov/spaceflight-qa/) — mission control's role communicating with astronauts in orbit.

These references support the operational vocabulary, not our fictional names, spacecraft or story. This document is an implementation direction; it does not prove every existing scene sign, objective and recording has been updated.
